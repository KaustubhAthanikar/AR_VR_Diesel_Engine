What is it?

A twin-cylinder, four-stroke diesel engine: two cylinders side by side share one crankshaft. Diesel engines ignite fuel by compressing air until it is very hot, so they need no spark plug.

The four strokes

  1. Intake: the piston moves down and the inlet valve lets air in.
  2. Compression: both valves close and the piston squeezes the air to a high pressure and temperature.
  3. Power: fuel is sprayed in, burns, and pushes the piston down.
  4. Exhaust: the exhaust valve opens and the piston pushes the burnt gas out.

One cycle takes two turns of the crankshaft. The camshaft turns at half crank speed to open the valves once per cycle.

How power flows

Fuel pump, injector, combustion, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, flywheel. The flywheel evens out the pulses so the engine runs smoothly.

Why two cylinders?

More power strokes per turn than a single cylinder, so less vibration and smoother torque, with a simple, compact layout.
